Abstract

We introduce Re2, a framework for high-quality offline outdoor scene relighting for 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) that combines physically based rendering (PBR) with video diffusion priors. Existing 3DGS relighting methods rely on inverse rendering to recover scene geometry and materials. However, 3DGS is optimized to reproduce input images under the observed lighting, often producing inaccurate geometry that does not generalize to new lighting, which creates severe relighting artifacts. Alternatively, 2D relighting methods can produce photorealistic images but lack global 3D scene awareness, resulting in physically inconsistent lighting and shadows. To overcome these limitations, Re2 leverages the complementary strengths of PBR and generative video priors, enabling photorealistic relighting while remaining grounded in the underlying 3D scene geometry. Specifically, we recover scene geometry and materials through inverse rendering and use our trained video diffusion model to correct its artifacts. To train this refinement model, we introduce a large-scale synthetic outdoor relighting dataset comprising ∼1.6 million images rendered under diverse HDR environment maps, with ground-truth geometry, materials, and illumination annotations. Our approach produces substantially higher-quality relit images, preserving fine scene details, recovering sharp cast shadows, and maintaining consistent appearance across views.

Method

Re² first reconstructs a physically based 3D Gaussian scene from multi-view captures, using geometry-aware initialization and explicit dominant-light sampling. For each view, it renders a 360° lighting sequence under the target HDR environment map and refines the imperfect PBR video with a video diffusion model by conditioning it with source input image. The refined frames are then used to optimize the final relit 3DGS.
